# Self-Healing Mortar with Superabsorbent Polymers

### A Fracture Mechanics Study of Dry and Calcium-Loaded SAP Systems

Experimental investigation of self-healing mortar incorporating superabsorbent polymers (SAP), evaluated through semi-circular bending (SCB) fracture testing.

This study compares conventional mortar with dry SAP and calcium-loaded (limewater-loaded) SAP systems to investigate how SAP influences crack resistance, fracture behavior, and post-damage performance.

> 🏆 Presented at the Symposium on Sustainable Construction Materials at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln — First Prize

---

## Overview

Cracking is one of the primary mechanisms that reduces the durability and service life of cementitious materials. Even relatively small cracks can allow water, chlorides, and other aggressive agents to penetrate the material, accelerating deterioration and increasing future repair requirements.

Superabsorbent polymers are capable of absorbing and later releasing water within the cementitious matrix. This behavior may support internal curing, continued hydration, crack sealing, and improved post-damage performance.

This project investigated three mortar systems:

- Control Mix — conventional mortar without SAP
- Dry SAP Mix — mortar containing dry SAP
- Calcium-Loaded SAP Mix — SAP pre-soaked in limewater before incorporation

The primary objective was not simply to determine which mixture had the highest strength, but to evaluate how each material behaved after controlled damage and subsequent healing.

---

## Research Questions

The study focused on several linked questions:

1. How does dry SAP affect early-age fracture behavior?
2. How does calcium-loaded SAP compare with conventional dry SAP?
3. How much load-carrying capacity remains after controlled cracking and healing?
4. How much fracture-energy capacity is retained after damage?
5. Do strength recovery and toughness recovery follow the same trend?

---

## Experimental Methodology

The experimental program used semi-circular bending testing to evaluate fracture response.

### Mixes

| Mix | Description |
|---|---|
| Control | Conventional mortar without SAP |
| Dry SAP | Mortar containing dry superabsorbent polymer |
| Calcium-Loaded SAP | SAP pre-soaked in limewater prior to mixing |
